\section*{EEG stream of the cap}

Depending on the settings of the manufacturer's software, the stream of the cap carries
only the 16 \ac{EEG} channels, in microvolts with a large DC offset, or 22 more columns:
a contact flag for every electrode, three accelerometer axes, a sample counter, a
streaming status and the battery level. The software selects the \ac{EEG} channels by
their labels, so it works with both.

\section*{Markers}

The calibration program and the speller \ac{GUI} send every event as a \ac{JSON}
object with the name of the event and its fields (\cref{tab:markers}). The speller
\ac{GUI} sends the same events as the calibration program; its first marker gives the
durations of the gaze shift and the feedback instead of the number of blocks and the
duration of the cue. It also sends three markers of its own, listed at the bottom of
the table.

\begin{table}[tb]
  \centering
  \caption{Markers of the calibration program and the speller \acs*{GUI}. Each is
    time-stamped with the flip that showed its event, or with the time it was sent if
    no flip shows it.}
  \label{tab:markers}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.6cm}X@{}}
    \toprule
    Event & Sent at & Fields \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{session\_start} & the start of the session & subject, frequencies, phases, refresh rate, number of blocks, cue and flicker durations, screen size, positions and size of the squares \\
    \texttt{block\_start}   & the start of a block & block \\
    \texttt{cue}            & the first flip of the cue & trial, block, target, frequency \\
    \texttt{stim\_on}       & the first flip of the flicker & trial, block, target, frequency \\
    \texttt{stim\_off}      & the first flip after the flicker & as \texttt{stim\_on}, plus the number of dropped frames and whether the flicker was complete \\
    \texttt{block\_end}     & the end of a block & block \\
    \texttt{session\_end}   & the end of the session & number of trials, whether the session was aborted \\
    \midrule
    \multicolumn{3}{@{}l}{\textit{Speller \acs*{GUI} only}} \\
    \texttt{state\_request} & every 0.5\,s until the first state of the backend arrives & none \\
    \texttt{feedback}       & the first flip of the feedback & trial, block, selected target, text \\
    \texttt{no\_answer}     & 3\,s after the flicker without an answer & trial, block \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\section*{Recording file}

A recording is one compressed NumPy~\citep{harris2020numpy} file with the fields of
\cref{tab:recording}.

\begin{table}[tb]
  \centering
  \caption{Contents of a recording file.}
  \label{tab:recording}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lX@{}}
    \toprule
    Field & Contents \\
    \midrule
    \ac{EEG}           & continuous signal of all columns of the stream, channels $\times$ samples, in \textmu V \\
    sample times       & \acs*{LSL} timestamp of every sample, in seconds \\
    markers            & the \acs*{JSON} marker strings as received \\
    marker times       & \acs*{LSL} timestamp of every marker, in seconds \\
    channels           & label of every column \\
    sampling rate      & nominal sampling rate of the stream, in Hz \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\section*{Logs of an online session}

Besides the recording, every online session leaves two logs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the trial table of the \ac{GUI}, with the timestamps of every trial, its
    dropped frames, the time of the answer, the selected target and the text, and
    with the timestamps of all flips saved beside it;
  \item the decision log of the backend, with the target, the decision, the selected
    item, the new text, the score of every target and the time taken by the
    classification and by the speller, and with the fusion also the prior and the
    posterior of every target.
\end{itemize}
