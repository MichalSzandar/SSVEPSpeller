\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

The motivation for this work stems from the need to improve communication options for
individuals with severe motor impairments. The system is designed specifically for
those with \ac{ALS} or locked-in syndrome. These individuals often retain their cognitive
function but cannot communicate effectively, because they have lost voluntary control of
their muscles. A \ac{BCI} can provide a means of communication by translating \ac{EEG}
signals into commands, enabling users to spell words and sentences without relying on
traditional input methods. The system is based on the \ac{SSVEP} paradigm, which is known
for its high \ac{ITR} and its robustness in \ac{BCI} applications. By integrating machine
learning and \ac{NLP} techniques, the system aims to enhance communication efficiency,
reduce user fatigue, and improve the overall user experience.

\section{Problem statement}
\label{sec:problem}

Several similar systems have been described in the literature, but most of them are
laboratory systems that depend on high-end \ac{EEG} equipment (64 channels with gel
electrodes) and do not use language models to enhance communication. My goal is to
develop a more accessible and practical \ac{BCI} system that can be used with
consumer-grade \ac{EEG} devices and uses language models to improve the speed and accuracy
of communication. This work addresses the challenge of creating a real-time \ac{BCI}
system that is both effective and user-friendly, and therefore suitable for individuals
with severe motor impairments who rely on such technology to communicate.

\section{Aim and scope}
\label{sec:aim}

The aim of this thesis is to design and implement a real-time \ac{BCI} system that
enables predictive communication based on \ac{EEG} signals. The system should accurately
detect which target the user is looking at by analysing the \ac{SSVEP} responses, and
integrate machine learning and \ac{NLP} techniques to make communication more efficient.

The system has a \ac{GUI} that shows the user a two-level menu with six flickering targets
on each level. The first level consists of six groups with six options each. The options
are the letters A--Z (English alphabet only), space, backspace and three word suggestions.
The user selects a group on the first level and then a letter or a word on the second
level.

The topic card of the thesis lists seven tasks, which also give the order of the chapters:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a review of the current literature on \ac{BCI} systems, \ac{SSVEP} paradigms and
        real-time \ac{EEG} signal processing methods (\cref{ch:background});
  \item the design and implementation of a \ac{GUI} with precise stimulation timing
        (\cref{ch:system});
  \item the implementation of an \ac{API} for real-time hardware integration and \ac{EEG}
        data acquisition (\cref{ch:system});
  \item the development of a signal processing pipeline, including artifact removal and
        temporal and spatial filtering (\cref{ch:processing});
  \item the training and evaluation of machine learning and statistical classification
        algorithms, such as \ac{CCA} and \ac{TRCA}, on offline data (\cref{ch:processing,ch:results});
  \item the integration of \ac{NLP} for real-time predictive text generation, to
        increase the \ac{ITR} (\cref{ch:language});
  \item system integration, online experiments and the evaluation of the overall
        performance of the system: accuracy and \ac{ITR} (\cref{ch:system,ch:results}).
\end{enumerate}

The system is gaze-based, which means that a user with motor impairments must still be
able to control their gaze. The system was tested online with only two subjects, and
offline also on the Benchmark dataset of 35 subjects~\citep{wang2017benchmark}.

\section{Use of AI tools}
\label{sec:ai-use}

AI tools were used primarily for researching the existing literature, reviewing and
summarising relevant papers, structuring, reviewing and writing tests for the implementation 
of the system. They were also used to fix typos and language in the thesis. The main tool was
Claude Code (Anthropic) with the Claude Opus 5.5 model. I used the AI tools to complement
my own work, and all sources of information obtained from AI tools have been properly
cited and acknowledged. I critically evaluated the outputs of the AI tools to ensure their
accuracy and relevance to the research objectives.
